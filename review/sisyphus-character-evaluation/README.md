# Sisyphus character candidate evaluation

The free Standard package was downloaded from [Quaternius on itch.io](https://quaternius.itch.io/universal-base-characters) on 2026-11-16. The included `License_Standard.txt` identifies the models as CC0 1.0. The original ZIP remains in the user's Downloads folder, SHA-256 `fdbf1804c90dfc1ea03e992bff7da2dfd1a79318e13270a660180f9308455f40`.

`superhero-male-untextured.gltf` is an evaluation-only derivative of `Base Characters/Godot - UE/Superhero_Male_FullBody.gltf` and its matching `.bin`: the binary is embedded as a data URI and texture references are removed. SHA-256 `4d39090358650ba2d3e7955aaf26b653f30243f0a46a43f163230360cd37daf8`. It is not loaded by the application. The original glTF refers to two normal-map names that are absent verbatim from its folder, so texture references need independent repair if the textured version is used.

Three.js GLTFLoader inspection found one skin and three skinned meshes: body 7,281 vertices / 12,566 triangles; eyes 556 / 768; eyebrows 646 / 984. The model has named pelvis, spine, arm, hand, leg and foot bones but no animation clips. At rest it is in a spread-arm pose, approximately 1.82 units tall. This is a candidate, not a completed Sisyphus figure: it needs suitable clothing, pushing pose and dynamic fitting of hands and feet against the rolling mesh and terrain, followed by visual and runtime checks. Do not replace the current figure until those are demonstrated.

A separate static pose experiment is available at `index.html` (serve the project with Vite). `pose-solver.js` uses cyclic coordinate descent against fixed arm and leg targets. In the measured test, hand bone errors were below 0.001 units; foot bone errors were approximately 0.017 and 0.044 units, and the lowest deformed body vertex was about 0.016 units below the reference floor. Those are bone-target measurements, not proof of palm-to-rock or sole-to-slope contact. The cloth is a temporary rigid shape; the figure is still too upright and has no adapted hair or animated climbing cycle. This preview must not be treated as a finished figure.

Update: the pose now uses a stronger torso lean and solves the middle-finger bones toward selected vertices of the preview rock rather than solving wrists to arbitrary points. The test bounds the finger-bone target errors; it does not prove skin-surface contact or exclude other hand vertices penetrating the rock. `hair-untextured.gltf` is a second evaluation derivative from the same CC0 Standard ZIP (`Hairstyles/Origin at 0/glTF (Godot)/Hair_SimpleParted.gltf` and its matching binary), with an embedded buffer and texture references removed; SHA-256 `d21cbcf5cd75c6e5744f7aa1a84068acbb0d6d017644b32c8bcb98be070e402c`. The preview attaches this hair to the head and the temporary rigid wrap to the pelvis before posing. A static screenshot shows the hair following the head; dynamic deformation and clothing remain unresolved.

Further update: the static pose preserves the feet's original world orientations and uses a shorter staggered stance. A deformed-mesh check selects vertices with more than 0.5 combined weight on each foot/ball bone (400 vertices per side): their lowest points are approximately +0.0003 and +0.0032 units above the flat floor. This is a sampled skin-weight classification and a static flat-ground result, not a guarantee that every sole vertex contacts a moving slope. It also does not measure hand surface penetration.

Hand-contact update: the preview solver offsets each finger target outward from a selected rock vertex. For the static convex preview rock, a triangle-plane check of 1,071 vertices per hand with more than 0.5 combined hand/finger bone influence found no vertices inside; the smallest maximum-plane separation was approximately 0.00085 units on each side. This is not a complete triangle/mesh intersection test and does not cover vertices below the skin-weight threshold, the application's irregular boulder, or motion.

Triangle and rotation update: convex clipping found no penetration among 3,388 triangles with at least one hand-dominant (>0.5 hand/finger influence) vertex in the zero-rotation preview pose. The solver now restores the original bone rotations before every solve and the preview slider rotates its test rock. Sampled angles 0, 0.25, -0.4, 0.7 and 0 again reach their offset finger targets; the last zero reproduces the initial arm orientation. The triangle-clearance check applies only at zero rotation, uses an interior tolerance, and excludes triangles without a hand-dominant vertex. At the slider's end the arms visually crowd each other, and target switching may cause jumps. The real boulder and moving slope are still not tested.

Continuity audit: sampling the current nearest-vertex target from -1 to 1 radians in 0.01-radian increments found a maximum left target jump of approximately 0.283 units at -0.02 radians. An experimental nearest-point-on-triangle target removed the vertex discretization but caused selected hand vertices to enter the preview rock even with an increased outward offset, so that experiment was reverted. A usable dynamic solver must jointly address contact orientation, continuous target selection and whole-hand clearance; the current slider remains exploratory.

Continuity update: a weighted blend of nearby preview-rock vertices replaces the hard nearest-vertex selection. For 0.01-radian samples over -1 to 1 radians, maximum observed left-target displacement is about 0.011 units (previously 0.283). The zero-angle selected hand vertices and triangles still pass the existing clearance test, but a separate nine-angle vertex audit found penetration at ±0.25 and ±0.75 radians, up to about 0.016 units by the convex-plane measure. The soft target is a pose experiment, not a validated contact solution or a claim of continuous collision-free motion.

A further rotation experiment uses a smooth angle-dependent offset and includes a reproducible `node review/sisyphus-character-evaluation/audit-contact.mjs` vertex audit. At 41 angles spaced 0.05 radians from -1 to 1, two samples still had selected hand vertices inside the convex test rock, with worst measured plane depth approximately 0.0056 units. Increasing the offset eliminated these sampled penetrations but caused a nearest-plane gap as large as 0.157 units, so the smaller offset was retained. The retained version can still have a visible gap (maximum sampled nearest-plane separation about 0.053); it is not a finished contact solution, and the audit does not cover triangles or unsampled angles.

Per-hand calibration update: the earlier combined nearest-plane statistic could hide one hand far from the rock; the audit now reports the worse of the two hands' nearest selected vertices. A reproducible grid search found separate left/right offsets at 21 nonnegative angles and the prototype interpolates them, mirroring sides for negative angles. On 201 samples, eight still had selected hand vertices inside (worst convex-plane depth approximately .0031); the largest per-hand nearest-plane separation was about .0235. This plane quantity is not a complete Euclidean surface distance. Larger safety bumps removed sampled penetration but worsened the existing continuity check, so the smaller bump is retained. The lookup is calibrated only for this convex test rock; whole-triangle clearance at nonzero angles, unsampled angles, the actual boulder and moving ground are not established.

Dense calibration update: `optimize-contact.mjs 100` searched offsets 0.08–0.29 in 0.005 increments at 101 nonnegative angles, independently per hand; the raw results are in `offset-search-dense.json`. The preview's two lookup arrays are a conservative upward envelope of those offsets (maximum adjacent change 0.009), mirrored between hands for negative angles, with a 0.003 interpolation bump. In a separate 1,001-angle audit, no selected hand-dominant vertices penetrated the convex preview rock beyond the 0.00001 tolerance; the largest per-hand nearest-plane statistic was approximately 0.0131. The zero-angle selected-triangle test and sampled target-continuity test pass. This is sampled and calibrated to this single static rig and test rock; it does not prove continuous-time collision freedom, surface distance, nonzero-angle triangle clearance, joint quality, clothing behavior, the irregular production boulder or slope motion.

Triangle audit update: `node review/sisyphus-character-evaluation/audit-contact.mjs 200 --triangles` clips 3,388 triangles with at least one hand-dominant vertex at each of 401 sampled angles. The first 201-angle pass revealed four angles with intersecting triangles even though selected vertices were outside. Adding 0.003 to both hands' calibrated lookup entries at indices 12 and 38 removed the detected intersections in the subsequent 401-angle pass; its largest per-hand nearest-plane statistic was approximately 0.0134. This only covers sampled angles, the selected triangles, a 0.00001 interior tolerance and the convex preview rock. Triangles without a hand-dominant vertex, continuous rotation, the production boulder, foot-to-slope contact and clothing remain unverified.

### 2026-11-16: Candidate garment pass

The isolated character preview now uses a procedurally shaped, pleated skirt with a belt, knot and hanging tie. The garment is rigidly attached to the pelvis; it is not skinned or cloth simulated, and intersections during motion have not been assessed. The preview uses a test rock and remains an experiment, not the application model or an L2 approval. Visual inspection confirmed that the knot and tie render with their intended material after correcting their missing vertex colors. The project test suite passed 115 tests; those tests do not validate this garment.
